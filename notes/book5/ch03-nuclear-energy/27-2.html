27.2 extension

Mass–energy relation

In every fission or fusion the products weigh slightly less than the reactants. The missing mass was not lost — it became energy, and the amount is fixed by \( E = \Delta m c^{2}\). This page keeps one picture (a balance) and one rule for converting mass and energy on the atomic scale.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • state mass-energy relationship \( \Delta E = \Delta m c^{2} \) extension

  • use atomic mass unit as a unit of energy extension

  • determine the energy release in nuclear reactions extension

  • apply \( \Delta E = \Delta m c^{2} \) to solve problems extension

AThe missing mass becomes energy

Mass–energy relation

In Einstein's special relativity, mass and energy are equivalent. Mass can be converted into energy according to

\[ E = \Delta m\, c^{2} \]

where \(E\) is the energy released, \(\Delta m\) is the mass defect (loss in mass) and \(c = 3 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\) is the speed of light in vacuum.

  • Because \(c^{2} \approx 9 \times 10^{16}\), even a tiny mass defect is a huge energy. For 1 g,
\[ E = (0.001)(3 \times 10^{8})^{2} = 9 \times 10^{13}\ \mathrm{J} \]
  • The “loss’’ happens in both fission and fusion. Splitting heavy nuclei and joining light ones both move toward more tightly bound nuclei (see binding-energy curve below); both end lighter.
  • The kinetic energy of the fragments plus gamma rays carries the released energy. In a reactor it becomes heat.

Concept check

(a) In nuclear fission the total mass of the products is larger than that of the reactants.

False. Products are lighter; the missing mass is the energy you count with \(E=\Delta m c^{2}\).

(b) In nuclear fission energy is released due to rapid collisions between nuclei.

False. Energy comes from the mass defect, not from “smashing’’ as a mechanical collision. Slow neutrons trigger fission without rapid collisions.

(c) Energy can be converted into matter.

True. The relation works both ways; pair production is an example, but the syllabus examines the mass→energy direction.

BSmaller units for smaller masses: eV and u

The joule and kilogram are far too large for nuclei. Two smaller units are used on the atomic scale.

UnitDefinitionIn SI
Electronvolt (eV)Energy gained by one electron crossing 1 V. \(1\ \mathrm{eV} = e \times 1\ \mathrm{V}\)\(1\ \mathrm{eV} = 1.60 \times 10^{-19}\ \mathrm{J}\). Often MeV = \(10^{6}\) eV.
Atomic mass unit (u)\(1\ \mathrm{u} = \tfrac{1}{12}\) × mass of a \(^{12}\mathrm{C}\) atom. By definition 1 mol of \(^{12}\mathrm{C}\) is 12 g, so \(1\ \mathrm{u} = \tfrac{0.012}{12 N_{A}}\)\(1\ \mathrm{u} = 1.661 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}\)

Because \(E = \Delta m c^{2}\), the two can be interchanged:

\[ 1\ \mathrm{u}\, c^{2} = (1.661 \times 10^{-27})(2.998 \times 10^{8})^{2} = 1.492 \times 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{J} = 931\ \mathrm{MeV} \]
\[ 1\ \mathrm{u} \equiv 931\ \mathrm{MeV} \qquad | \qquad 1\ \mathrm{MeV}=1.60 \times 10^{-13}\ \mathrm{J} \]
  • On a calculation, keep masses in u until the final step, then multiply by 931 to get MeV; to get joules, multiply MeV by \(1.60 \times 10^{-13}\). Or convert u to kg first (\(\times 1.661 \times 10^{-27}\)) and use \(c^{2}\).
  • Typical masses: proton 1.007276 u, neutron 1.008665 u, He-4 nucleus 4.001506 u, electron 0.000549 u. The syllabus gives these where needed; do not memorize.
  • Watch the particle: the table gives nuclear masses in some problems, atomic masses in others (the latter includes the electrons). The difference cancels only if both sides include the same number of electrons.
Worked example

Mass equivalent of 1000 MeV? (Given \(c = 3.00 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\), \(1\ \mathrm{u}=931\) MeV.)

  1. \(1000\ \mathrm{MeV} = 1000/931 = 1.07\ \mathrm{u}\). In kg: \(1.07 \times 1.661 \times 10^{-27} = 1.78 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}\).
  2. Or directly: \(m = E/c^{2} = (1000 \times 10^{6} \times 1.60 \times 10^{-19})/(9 \times 10^{16}) = 1.78 \times 10^{-27}\ \mathrm{kg}\).

Concept check

The masses of a helium-4 nucleus, a proton and a neutron are 4.001506 u, 1.007276 u and 1.008665 u. How much mass is converted to energy when two protons and two neutrons fuse into one He-4 nucleus?

Mass before = \(2(1.007276+1.008665)=4.031882\) u; after = 4.001506 u; defect = 0.030376 u, B (28.3 MeV). A is half the value; D adds instead of subtracts.

CWorking the energy of any reaction

Method

1. Add the masses before. 2. Add the masses after. 3. Subtract → defect \(\Delta m\) (in u). 4. Energy = \(\Delta m \times 931\) MeV; × \(1.60 \times 10^{-13}\) → J. Watch nucleon number: balance \(A\) to find an unknown neutron count.

Worked example — fusion (Example 27.3)

\( {}^{2}_{1}\mathrm{H} + {}^{3}_{1}\mathrm{H} \rightarrow {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He} + {}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n}\). Given masses: D 2.0136 u, T 3.0155 u, He-4 4.0015 u, neutron 1.0087 u. (a) Why high T? (b) Energy in MeV. (c) 5 g of equal D+T — reactions and total J.

  1. (a) Nuclei must overcome Coulomb repulsion; only at \(10^{7}\) K is the kinetic energy enough.
  2. (b) \(\Delta m = (2.0136+3.0155)-(4.0015+1.0087)=0.0189\) u. \(E = 0.0189 \times 931 = 17.6\) MeV (\(2.82 \times 10^{-12}\) J).
  3. (c) Mass per reaction = \((2.0136+3.0155) \times 1.661 \times 10^{-27}=8.35 \times 10^{-27}\) kg. Reactions in 5 g = \(0.005/8.35e-27 = 5.99 \times 10^{23}\). Total \(E = 17.6\ \mathrm{MeV} \times 5.99e23 = 1.05 \times 10^{25}\) MeV = \(1.7 \times 10^{12}\) J.
Worked example — fission (Example 27.4)

\( {}^{235}_{92}\mathrm{U} + {}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} \rightarrow {}^{148}_{57}\mathrm{La} + {}^{85}_{35}\mathrm{Br} + x\,{}^{1}_{0}\mathrm{n} + 166.37\ \mathrm{MeV}\). Masses: U-235 234.9934 u, La-148 147.9009 u, Br-85 84.8964 u.

  1. (a) Nucleon balance: \(235+1=148+85+x\) → \(x=3\).
  2. (b) Defect \(= 166.37/931 = 0.1787\) u. Balance masses: \(234.9934 + m_{n} = 147.9009+84.8964+3m_{n}+0.1787\) → \(m_{n}=1.0087\) u.
  3. (c) Mass of one U-235 = \(234.9934 \times 1.661e-27 = 3.90 \times 10^{-25}\) kg. Nuclei in 5 g = \(0.005/3.90e-25 = 1.28 \times 10^{22}\). Power = \(166.37\ \mathrm{MeV} \times 1.28e22\) reactions per s = \(3.4 \times 10^{11}\) W (340 GW thermal; electricity is less after efficiency).

Compare: single fission 166 MeV > single fusion 17.6 MeV, but per kilogram fusion gives more — the textbook ratio is about 5:1, because the light reactants pack more reactions per gram.

Worked example — coal vs uranium (Example 27.2)

0.08% of a U-235 nucleus's mass becomes energy. (a) Energy from 1 kg complete fission. (b) Coal needed for same energy (1 kg coal = \(2.8 \times 10^{7}\) J).

  1. (a) \(E = 0.0008 \times 1 \times c^{2} = 0.0008 \times 9e16 = 7.2 \times 10^{13}\) J.
  2. (b) Coal mass = \(7.2e13/2.8e7 = 2.6 \times 10^{6}\) kg (∼2600 tonnes). One kilogram of uranium replaces a lorries-load of coal.

Concept check

A nucleus of mass \(m_{1}\) reacts with two nuclei of mass \(m_{2}\) to form three nuclei each of mass \(m_{3}\). The energy given out is

C: before = \(m_{1}+2m_{2}\), after = \(3m_{3}\), defect = before − after. D is the sign reversed.

Concept check

A proton mass is 1.007276 u. If it were completely converted to energy, which is correct?

\(1\ \mathrm{u} ≡ 931\) MeV, so 1.007 u ≈ 938 MeV, B. A confuses u with MeV numerically; C/D are unit slips.

DA device that runs on α-decay: the RTG

Worked example — RTG (Example 27.5)

\( {}^{238}_{94}\mathrm{Pu} \rightarrow {}^{234}_{92}\mathrm{U} + {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He}\). Masses: Pu-238 237.9980 u, U-234 233.9905 u, α 4.0015 u.

  1. (a) \(\Delta m = 237.9980-(233.9905+4.0015)=0.0060\) u. \(E = 0.0060 \times 931 = 5.59\) MeV = \(8.94 \times 10^{-13}\) J.
  2. (b) If that becomes KE of the α: \(E = \tfrac12 m_{\alpha} v^{2}\) with \(m_{\alpha}=4.0015 \times 1.661e-27\) kg → \(v \approx 1.64 \times 10^{7}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}}\).
  3. (c) Electrical output 300 W at 10% conversion: \(300 = 0.10 \times E \times A\) → \(A = 3.36 \times 10^{14}\) Bq. Mass needed ≈ \((A/k) \times (M/N_{A})\) — a few kg for a space probe.
  4. (d) Activity \(A = kN\) falls as \(N = N_{0}e^{-kt}\). As nuclei decay, fewer decays per second → less energy per second → power drops with the same half-life.
  • RTG is not a reactor: no chain, no control rods, no critical mass. It is a decaying source whose power is proportional to its activity.
  • Voyager 1 (launched with \(3.2 \times 10^{24}\) Pu-238 atoms, half-life 87.74 y) is the textbook Ch.10 case: launch power \(\sim 470\) W thermal, \(\sim 150\) W electric; after 36 y the remaining power is \((1/2)^{36/87.74} \approx 75\)% of launch — exactly the exponential fall shown above.
  • Handling: α is stopped by the metal casing, so external dose is negligible — a worker can hold the sealed generator with bare hands. Internally it would be lethal, which is why it is sealed.

Concept check

A plutonium RTG delivers 300 W electrical at 10% conversion. The energy per α-decay is \(8.9 \times 10^{-13}\) J. Its activity is about

\(A = P/(0.10E) = 300/(0.10 \times 8.9e-13) = 3.4e14\), B. A forgets the 10%; C uses 1%.

EBinding energy — why both directions release energy

The textbook explains both fission and fusion with one curve: the average binding energy per nucleon \(B/A\) against mass number \(A\).

  • Heavy side (> Fe): fission into two medium nuclei → higher \(B/A\) → mass defect. U-235 fission product distribution peaks around \(A \sim 95\) and \(140\).
  • Light side (< Fe): fusion of light nuclei → higher \(B/A\) → mass defect. D+T → He-4 climbs steeply up the curve, so energy per nucleon is larger than for fission (≈4 MeV per nucleon fused vs ≈0.9 MeV per nucleon split) — hence fusion wins per kilogram even though one fission gives more MeV than one fusion.
  • Nuclei heavier than Pb-208 (\(A=208\)) are unstable (half-lives finite) and can α-decay toward the peak.
Geothermal snapshot (not examinable)

About half of Earth's internal heat is from radioactive decay (mainly U, Th, K-40) in the core and mantle. Hot springs and geothermal power stations are literally decay heat arriving at the surface.

Concept check

Heavier nuclei beyond iron tend to undergo fission, lighter nuclei below iron tend to undergo fusion. Why?

B is the binding-energy argument. A is true but not the reason; C/D are invented.
