3Pressurized-water reactor — label it

PartWhat you must say in the exam
Fuel rodsContain U-235 (~3%) where fission occurs; heat comes from fragment kinetic energy
Control rodsAbsorb neutrons → in = slower chain, out = faster. Not “slow down neutrons” — that is the moderator's job
Moderator / pressurized water circuitSlows neutrons to the slow speeds U-235 needs; carries heat to boiler
Boiler / steam generator, turbine, generatorHeat → steam → turbines → electricity. About 30–40% efficiency to electricity
  • Enrichment: bomb >90%, reactor ~3%, natural 0.7%. Hence a reactor cannot explode like a bomb — the physics (critical mass at that concentration) for a runaway chain is not there.
  • Chernobyl: chain got out of control and vessel exploded. Fukushima: chain had already stopped; decay heat melted the fuel when cooling failed — still a meltdown, but not a nuclear explosion.

4Binding energy in one picture

The average binding energy per nucleon \\(B/A\\) peaks at iron-56 (~8.8 MeV). Moving toward Fe releases energy: light nuclei fuse up, heavy nuclei split down. Energy per nucleon fused (≈4 MeV) exceeds per nucleon split (≈0.9 MeV) — hence fusion wins per kilogram even though one fission gives more MeV.

  • Right of Fe → fission favored; left of Fe → fusion favored. Nuclei beyond Pb-208 are unstable.
  • Mass defect = (mass of separate nucleons) − (mass of bound nucleus). The defect × 931 MeV is the binding energy.
  • Half the Earth's internal heat is decay heat (U, Th, K-40) — geothermal energy is literally nuclear energy arriving at the surface.

5Traps that cost marks

A chain needs ≥1 neutron per fission onward

2–3 neutrons emitted ≠ 2–3 more fissions. If each fission triggers <1 on average, the chain dies even though neutrons exist. Critical mass is about concentration, not count.

Slow neutrons for fission, heat for fusion

Fast neutrons cannot trigger U-235 fission — they must be slowed by the moderator. Fusion needs \\(10^{7}\\) K, not a neutron. Swapping the conditions loses the mark.

Control rods absorb, moderator slows

“Inserting rods slows neutrons’’ is a confusion. Rods absorb neutrons; water slows them. Give the right part the right verb.

Mass defect is a tiny fraction

~0.1% of mass becomes energy in fission (≈0.08% in the textbook example), not “most of the mass”. 1 g → \\(9\\times10^{13}\\) J is huge because \\(c^{2}\\) is huge.

Per reaction ≠ per kilogram

“Fusion releases more than fission’’ is only true per kilogram. Per reaction fission (≈170 MeV) exceeds D+T fusion (≈18 MeV). Read which is asked.

RTG power follows activity

A plutonium generator is not a reactor: no chain, no critical mass. Its electrical power falls as \\(P\\propto A = kN\\), with half-life 87.74 y. After one half-life it is 50%.

Reactor cannot become a bomb

Power-reactor fuel at ~3% cannot sustain the supercritical growth of a bomb (>90%). A meltdown is overheating by decay heat, not a nuclear detonation.

1 u is not a neutron's mass

Neutron is 1.008665 u, proton 1.007276 u, He-4 nucleus 4.001506 u. 1 u is \\(1/12\\) of C-12. Use the data given; keep nuclear vs atomic masses consistent.

6Last checks

Concept check

A heavy nucleus is split into two lighter ones in a reaction that emits neutrons and releases energy. This is

D. Heavy → lighter with neutrons is fission. Fusion is light → heavier; α/β are single-particle decays.

Concept check

When a \\({}^{235}\\mathrm{U}\\) nucleus undergoes the fission \\({}^{235}\\mathrm{U}+\\mathrm{n}\\rightarrow{}^{141}\\mathrm{Ba}+{}^{92}\\mathrm{Kr}+3\\mathrm{n}+E\\), the total mass after is

C. Measured masses before exceed after by about 0.2 u; the missing mass becomes kinetic energy and photons. D confuses the counted neutrons (already in the mass balance) with the defect.

Concept check

The Sun's power comes from

B — D–D and D–T cycles in the core. The other options are textbook distractors.
