Ionizing radiation

ATwo types of radiation

Definition

Radiation is energy travelling from one place to another, either as an electromagnetic wave or as a stream of fast particles. It does not need a medium: light and X-rays cross a vacuum, and so do α and β particles.

Electromagnetic radiationParticle radiation
Oscillating E and B fields; no mass, no charge; speed \( c = 3 \times 10^{8}\ \mathrm{m\,s^{-1}} \) in vacuumMoving particles with mass and kinetic energy; may carry charge; speed below \( c \)
Radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-rays, γ raysElectron beams, α particles, β particles, neutrons
Both transfer energy. Both are radiation.

Concept check

Which of the following statements about radiation is/are correct?

  1. Radiation always requires a medium to travel in.
  2. Radiation transfers mass from one place to another.
  3. All electromagnetic waves are a form of radiation.

(3) is the definition: every EM wave carries energy from one place to another. (1) is false because EM waves and nuclear particles cross a vacuum; sunlight reaches us through empty space. (2) is false as a general statement: an EM wave transfers energy but no mass, and even a particle beam is described as radiation because of the energy it carries, not the mass.

BIonizing means knocking an electron out

Definition

Ionizing radiation carries enough energy per photon or per particle to remove an electron from an atom or molecule, turning it into an ion. Radiation that cannot do this is non-ionizing.

IonizingNon-ionizing
Energy per photon or particleEnough to knock an electron out of an atom or moleculeNot enough; at most the electron is raised to a higher energy level and falls back
ExamplesX-rays, γ rays, most ultraviolet; α and β particlesRadio, microwaves, infrared, visible light, the lowest-energy part of ultraviolet
HazardIons and broken bonds inside cells damage tissue and DNA; cells may die or become cancerousHeating at high intensity only; no ionization

Concept check

Which of the following statements about ionizing radiation is/are correct?

  1. Its energy is high enough to strike electrons out of atoms or molecules.
  2. It is radiation in the form of ions.
  3. X-rays are ionizing radiation.

(1) is the definition. (3) is true: X-ray photons carry far more energy than the few eV needed to free an electron. (2) is the common slip: "ionizing" describes what the radiation does to matter, not what it is made of. X-rays and γ rays carry no charge at all and still ionize; a beam of ions would be something else entirely.

CWhere the electromagnetic spectrum becomes ionizing

radiomicroIRvisUVX-raysGamma ray
non-ionizing split ionizing EM
  • Energy per photon rises with frequency (and falls with wavelength). Only photons above the ionization threshold can knock an electron out, so the ionizing part of the spectrum is the high-frequency, short-wavelength end.
  • Radio waves, microwaves, infrared and visible light are non-ionizing. The boundary lies inside the ultraviolet band; radiation of higher frequency than ultraviolet, that is X-rays and γ rays, is ionizing.
  • Scale to remember: visible light \( \lambda \sim 5 \times 10^{-7}\ \mathrm{m} \); X-rays \( \lambda \sim 10^{-11} \) to \( 10^{-8}\ \mathrm{m} \), \( f \sim 10^{18}\ \mathrm{Hz} \); γ rays are shorter still, \( f \sim 10^{20}\ \mathrm{Hz} \). Short wavelength goes with high photon energy, hence both ionizing and highly penetrating.

Concept check

Which of the following properties are common to X-rays and to nuclear radiation (α, β, γ)?

  1. ionizing
  2. emitted spontaneously
  3. emitted from the nucleus
  4. invisible

Both are ionizing and both are invisible, so (1) and (4). (2) fails for X-rays: a tube emits only while its supply is on, so the emission is controlled, not spontaneous. (3) fails too: X-rays come from electrons decelerating in a metal target, outside any nucleus. These two differences (origin and spontaneity) are the whole distinction between an X-ray and a γ ray, which are otherwise the same kind of wave; see Section G.

DHow an X-ray tube makes X-rays

Part of the tubeWhat it does
Heated filament (cathode)Emits electrons when hot, so a current can flow across the tube.
Very high voltage (tens of kV) between cathode and targetAccelerates the electrons to very high speed. A higher voltage gives electrons more kinetic energy and so more penetrating X-rays.
Vacuum inside the glassElectrons must not lose energy by colliding with air molecules on the way.
Heavy metal target (e.g. tungsten), angledElectrons are stopped abruptly; part of their kinetic energy is emitted as X-rays, which leave through the side of the tube.
Cooling (oil, rotating target)Only about 1% of the electrons' kinetic energy becomes X-rays; the rest heats the target.
Properties of X-rays

X-rays are electromagnetic waves of very short wavelength (about \( 10^{-10}\ \mathrm{m} \)). They carry no charge and no mass, travel at \( c \), are not deflected by electric or magnetic fields, pass through soft materials but are absorbed by dense ones, ionize atoms, blacken photographic film and cause some materials to fluoresce.

Concept check

(a) A beam of X-rays is a stream of fast-moving electrons.

The electrons stop in the target. What leaves the tube is an electromagnetic wave produced by the electrons' kinetic energy; it carries no charge, so an electric field cannot deflect it.

(b) X-rays are produced by bombarding a metal target with high-frequency electromagnetic waves.

The projectile is a fast electron, not a wave. The syllabus statement is "the emission of X-rays when fast electrons hit a heavy metal target".

(c) In the tube the heavy metal target is connected to the negative terminal of the high-voltage supply.

Electrons are negative, so to be pulled towards the target the target must be the positive terminal (the anode). The hot filament that emits them is the negative terminal (the cathode). X-rays leave from the target, not from the filament.

Write it out

Describe briefly how X-rays are produced in an X-ray tube. (3 marks)

  1. A filament is heated and emits electrons (thermionic emission).
  2. A very high voltage between the filament and a heavy metal target accelerates the electrons to a high speed across the evacuated tube.
  3. When the fast electrons hit the target they decelerate abruptly and part of their kinetic energy is emitted as X-rays (the rest becomes heat in the target).

Markers look for the three ideas in order: electron source, acceleration by a high voltage, fast electrons stopped by a heavy metal target. "Heated filament" alone or "electrons hit metal" alone scores one mark.

EUses of X-rays

UseWhich property is used
Medical radiography: bone fractures, teeth, chest; CT scans build a 3D image from many anglesLower-frequency X-rays pass through flesh but are absorbed by bone and teeth, so bone appears white on the developed film and flesh dark
Industrial inspection of rails, welds, castings and pipes for hidden cracks; checking diamondsHigher-frequency X-rays penetrate even bone and metal; a crack lets more through and shows as a darker line
Security screening of luggage and people at airportsPenetrating power differs with density: dense objects cast a shadow (some scanners image the reflected X-rays instead, so metal shows black)
Radiotherapy: killing cancer cells with a focused high-energy beamIonizing: X-rays damage the DNA of the cells they pass through
Reading an X-ray image

Everything follows from one rule: the film blackens where X-rays reach it. The more a material absorbs, the whiter its shadow.

  • A metal pin holding a broken bone appears whiter than the bone: metal absorbs even more X-rays than bone.
  • A decayed (hollow) part of a tooth appears darker than healthy tooth: less material, so more X-rays get through. A metal filling appears white.
  • A pair of scissors placed on a wrapped film: the bare film goes darkest, the plastic handles leave a grey shadow, the steel blades leave a white shadow.
Exam tip: safe use

Because X-rays are ionizing, every use comes with precautions and you may be asked for them: confirm the patient is not pregnant (a foetus is easily damaged; ultrasound is used instead); remove metal objects, which block X-rays and spoil the image; use the lowest exposure and photon energy that still produce the required image; cover other parts of the body with a lead apron; the radiographer stands behind a lead screen or leaves the room with the door closed, and only essential staff are present.

Concept check

Which statement about X-rays is incorrect?

C is the false statement: an electric field only deflects charged particles, and X-rays carry no charge. A is how the tube works, B is their nature, and D is the key subtlety: ionization does not require the radiation itself to be charged, it requires enough energy per photon to remove an electron.

Concept check

In which set-up are X-rays produced?

Two conditions are needed: fast electrons as the projectile and a heavy metal target that stops them abruptly. A and B use a wave, not electrons, so they can at most warm or ionize the surface. C has electrons but a light, soft target that slows them gradually and produces essentially no X-rays.

GNuclear radiation is spontaneous; an X-ray beam is switched

Nuclear radiation (α, β, γ)X-rays
SourceThe nucleus of an unstable atom (a radioactive substance)Fast electrons stopped by a metal target in a tube
ControlSpontaneous and random: cannot be switched off, sped up or slowed down by heating, pressure or chemical changeSwitched on and off with the tube's power supply; intensity and penetrating power set by the current and voltage
Natureα: helium nucleus (2p + 2n). β: fast electron. γ: very high-frequency EM waveEM wave, like γ but produced outside the nucleus and usually of lower frequency
In commonAll are ionizing and invisible. γ rays and X-rays in particular are both EM waves: both travel at \( c \), cross a vacuum, carry no charge (so no field deflects them), and blacken photographic film. The only difference between a γ ray and an X-ray is its origin.
Definition

A substance is radioactive if it spontaneously emits α, β or γ radiation from unstable nuclei. The emission is called radioactive decay, and the radiation is called nuclear radiation because it comes from the nucleus. Becquerel discovered it in 1896 when uranium salts blackened a wrapped photographic plate, exactly as X-rays do. The names α, β and γ were given before their nature was known; Section 25.2 explains what each one is.

Exam tip

If a question says radiation from a radioactive source passes undeflected through a field, it is γ, never "X-rays": a radioactive source does not emit X-rays, because X-rays are not nuclear radiation.

Concept check

(a) All types of nuclear radiation are electromagnetic waves.

Only γ is an EM wave. α particles are helium nuclei and β particles are electrons; both have mass and charge.

(b) The emission of radiation from uranium can be switched on and off like an X-ray tube.

Decay is spontaneous. Nothing done to the atom from outside (heat, pressure, chemical reaction, electric field) changes when or whether its nucleus decays.

(c) After a nuclear accident, the "radiation" that the wind carries to neighbouring countries is α, β and γ radiation.

The radiation itself is energy (particles or waves) travelling in straight lines from the nucleus for at most a few metres in air (α and β) or until absorbed (γ). What wind can spread is radioactive material, the dust that contains the unstable nuclei, which then emits radiation wherever it lands. Keep "radiation" and "radioactive substance" separate.

Concept check

Which of the following is/are a difference between γ rays and X-rays?

  1. γ rays are ionizing but X-rays are not.
  2. γ rays are emitted from nuclei but X-rays are not.
  3. X-rays are electromagnetic waves but γ rays are not.

Only (2) is a real difference. Both are ionizing EM waves travelling at \( c \); a γ photon and an X-ray photon of the same frequency are indistinguishable. What differs is where they come from: a γ ray leaves an excited nucleus during radioactive decay (spontaneous), an X-ray is produced when fast electrons are stopped in a metal target (switchable).

Concept check

Which of the following nuclear reactions is/are spontaneous?

  1. β decay of sodium-24
  2. a boron nucleus absorbing an incoming neutron
  3. fusion of hydrogen isotopes in a reactor

"Spontaneous" means the nucleus changes on its own with nothing coming in. Only (1) qualifies: radioactive decay. (2) needs an incoming neutron and (3) needs the nuclei to be forced together at enormous temperature; both are induced reactions that stop when the supply of neutrons or heat stops.
