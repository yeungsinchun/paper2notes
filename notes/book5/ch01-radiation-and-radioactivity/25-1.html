25.1

Ionizing radiation

Energy on the move

Fig. 25.2

Radiation = energy flowing from one place to another.

Carrier: EM wave, or particles that carry kinetic energy.

Ionizing means knockout

Fig. 25.3

Ionizing radiation has enough energy to knock an electron out of an atom or molecule.

Hazard: damaged tissue / DNA.

Concept check

What does ionizing radiation do to an atom?

Where the EM cut sits

Fig. 25.5

For EM waves: higher frequency → more energy.

Book cut: frequencies higher than ultraviolet, i.e. X-rays and gamma rays, are ionizing.

X-ray tube

Fig. 25.6

High-speed electrons hit a heavy metal target. Some KE becomes X-rays.

X-rays: uncharged EM, travel at c, blacken film, ionizing.

Concept check

(a) A beam of X-rays is a stream of fast-moving electrons.

(b) X-rays are produced by bombarding a metal target with high-frequency EM waves.

(c) Exposure to X-rays is dangerous because they can damage cells.

Flesh transmits, bone absorbs

Fig. 25.7

Soft tissue transmits → film blackens. Bone absorbs → bone looks white on the developed film.

Nuclear radiation vs an X-ray switch

25.1 C

Radioactive substances emit from the nucleus.

Names α, β, γ were arbitrary at discovery.

Decay is spontaneous and random: you cannot control time or direction the way you switch an X-ray tube.
