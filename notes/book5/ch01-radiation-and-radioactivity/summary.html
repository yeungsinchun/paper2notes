Chapter 25

Summary: Radiation and Radioactivity

The whole chapter on one page: the facts to know cold, the equations to write, and the traps examiners set.

Learning objectives

Students should be able to:

  • realise X-rays as ionizing electromagnetic radiations of short wavelengths with high penetrating power

  • realise the emission of X-rays when fast electrons hit a heavy metal target

  • discuss the uses of X-rays

  • describe the origin and nature of α, β and γ radiations

  • compare α, β and γ radiations in terms of their penetrating power, ranges, ionizing power, behaviour in electric field and magnetic field, and cloud chamber tracks

  • realise the occurrence of radioactive decay in unstable nuclides

  • realise the existence of background radiation

  • detect radiation with a photographic film and GM counter

  • detect radiation in terms of count rate using a GM counter

  • suggest safety precautions in handling radioactive sources

  • describe the structure of an atom

  • define atomic number as the number of protons in the nucleus and mass number as the sum of the number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom

  • use symbolic notations to represent nuclides

  • define isotope

  • realise the existence of radioactive isotopes in some elements

  • represent radioactive transmutations in α, β and γ decays using equations

3Notation and equations

\[ {}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}: \quad A = Z + N, \quad N = A - Z \]
\[ {}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \rightarrow {}^{A-4}_{Z-2}\mathrm{Y} + {}^{4}_{2}\mathrm{He} \quad (\alpha) \]
\[ {}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z+1}\mathrm{Y} + {}^{0}_{-1}e \quad (\beta) \]
\[ {}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X}^{*} \rightarrow {}^{A}_{Z}\mathrm{X} + \gamma \quad (\gamma) \]
\[ 4n_{\alpha} = \Delta A, \qquad 2n_{\alpha} - n_{\beta} = \Delta Z \]
\[ \text{corrected count rate} = \text{recorded} - \text{background} \]
  • Balance top and bottom separately: total \( A \) and total \( Z \) are the same on both sides. Emitted particles go on the right.
  • Every member of a decay series has \( A \) differing from the parent's by a multiple of 4; the end product is the lead isotope that satisfies this.
  • Isotopes: same \( Z \), different \( A \) (different \( N \)). Same chemistry, different mass. Not "same \( A \)".

5Traps that cost marks

A drop at 5 mm Al is not "β only"

Aluminium stops α as well as β. You need the paper step to rule out α and the lead step (a reading at background) to rule out γ.

Lead halves γ; it never zeroes it

Each 25 mm of lead halves the γ. A reading can never fall below background, and a reading that keeps changing with thickness is not background.

β bends more because of mass, not force

The force on an α is twice as large. The β is deflected more because it is about 7200 times lighter.

The β electron comes from the nucleus

A neutron becomes a proton and an electron. It is not an orbital electron, and no neutron is emitted.

Ions do the work, not the α

A charged object near an α source is neutralised by ions of the opposite sign drawn out of the ionized air. Moving the source a few centimetres away ends the effect.

X-rays are not nuclear radiation

Undeflected radiation from a radioactive source is γ. X-rays and γ differ only in origin; both are ionizing EM waves.

Irradiated is not contaminated

An X-rayed patient or a wrapped film under a source has absorbed energy and is not radioactive. Only radioactive material on or in an object makes it radioactive.

"α particle", not "α decay"

When asked to state the kind of radiation, name the particle. A radium source also gives β and γ because its daughters are radioactive, not because it is impure.

6Last checks

Concept check

A source emitting α, β and γ radiation is placed in a vacuum in a uniform magnetic field directed into the page. Three paths are seen: P curves slightly one way, Q is straight, R curves sharply the other way. Which identification is correct?

The undeflected path must be the uncharged γ, so Q = γ (A, C, D all give Q a charged particle). Of the two charged radiations the light β curves sharply, so R = β and P = α: B. The two curves are on opposite sides because the charges have opposite signs.

Concept check

A nuclide P decays by α emission to Q, and Q decays by β emission to R. Which of the following statements is/are correct?

  1. P and R have the same number of neutrons.
  2. R has one more proton than Q.
  3. The mass numbers of Q and R are equal.

Let P be \( (Z, N) \). Q = \( (Z-2, N-2) \); R = \( (Z-1, N-3) \). (1) is false: R has three fewer neutrons than P. (2) is true: β adds a proton. (3) is true: β leaves \( A \) unchanged. Answer C.

Concept check

Which of the following would reduce the count rate registered by a GM tube placed 10 cm from a β source in air?

  1. inserting a 5 mm aluminium sheet between source and tube
  2. applying a strong magnetic field across the path
  3. inserting a sheet of paper between source and tube

(1) stops the β. (2) deflects the β off the line to the tube, so fewer arrive. (3) does nothing measurable: paper stops α, and β passes through it. Answer D. (Reversing the polarity of deflecting plates would also do it; distance already exceeds the α range, so α plays no part.)
