## 25.1 Ionizing radiation (printed pp.4–10)

### A. What counts as radiation, and what is ionizing

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.2 (light vs electron beam) as two icons: EM wave vs particle KE. Fig. 25.3 knockout animation. Fig. 25.5 spectrum with ionizing cut after UV.
- **Text (minimal):** Radiation = energy flow (waves or particles). Ionizing = energy high enough to knock electrons out. X-rays and γ are ionizing EM. Hazard: tissue / DNA damage (Fig. 25.4 is optional still).
- **Check:** Textbook Checkpoint 1 Q1 T/F (X-ray is not a beam of electrons; production is electrons on metal, not EM on metal). QB `PHY15011101`.

### B. X-rays: production and imaging

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.6 tube animation. Fig. 25.7 flesh vs bone vs film. Optional: scissors darkening rank from the 25.1 exercise.
- **Text:** Produced when high-speed electrons hit a heavy metal target (KE → X-rays). Soft tissue transmits, bone absorbs → white bone on film. Neutral, travel at c, blacken film. Medical vs security vs industrial uses; Röntgen history is optional sidebar.
- **Check:** HKDSE 2022/31 (which X-ray statement is incorrect). Textbook exercise Q2 (UV vs electron on sponge vs metal).

### C. Nuclear radiation vs X-rays

- **Visual:** Three-type strip: α = He nucleus, β = electron, γ = high-f EM. Contrast: X-ray tube is switched; decay is spontaneous and random.
- **Text:** Radioactive substances emit from the **nucleus**. Names α, β, γ were arbitrary at discovery. Cannot control time or direction. History (Becquerel / Curie) optional.
- **Check:** Checkpoint 2 T/F (not all nuclear radiation is EM; cannot control like an X-ray tube; radiation is not “spread by wind” as a beam). HKDSE 2015/31 (which reactions are spontaneous).

## 25.2 Origin of nuclear radiation (printed pp.11–26)

### A. Atomic model, nuclide, isotope

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.11 atom with scale callout. Table 25.1 as a tiny comparison. Fig. 25.12 notation. Table 25.2 four Bohr sketches. Fig. 25.13 hydrogen isotopes.
- **Text:** Nucleus = protons + neutrons (nucleons). Neutral atom: Z electrons = Z protons. A = Z + N. Nuclide = given A and Z. Isotopes: same Z, different N. Strong nuclear force (enrichment) - short-range; Z > 83 unstable. Puzzle “pack the human race” optional.
- **Check:** Checkpoint after atomic model (complete the table; T/F on empty space and isotopes). HKDSE 2012/36 (N–A plot for one element).

### B. Radioactive decay and transmutation

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.14 overview. Fig. 25.15 α of ²⁰⁸Po. Fig. 25.16 β of ⁶⁰Co. Fig. 25.17 γ of ⁶⁰Ni*. Fig. 25.18 U-238 on A–Z and N–Z.
- **Text / equations (keep on-screen, not prose):**
  - α: ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ⁻⁴_Z₋₂ Y + ⁴₂He
  - β⁻: ᴬ_Z X → ᴬ_Z₊₁ Y + ⁰₋₁e  (electron from nucleus, not an orbiting electron)
  - γ: ᴬ_Z X* → ᴬ_Z X + γ  (not a transmutation; A, Z unchanged)
  - Decay series until a stable nuclide. γ does not move the A–Z / N–Z point.
- **Worked examples in book:** 25.2 Ra → Rn + α; 25.3 C-14 β; 25.4 thorium series A–Z sketch.
- **Check:** HKDSE 2013/34, 2014/31, 2020/31, 2021/31, 2025/32. LQ 2026/12(a)(i) decay equations only.

### C. Sources, lab handling, background

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.19 sealed source. Warning trefoil. Fig. 25.21–25.23 as a simple pie (natural ~80%, artificial ~20%; radon largest slice). Do not copy photo collages.
- **Text:** Sealed lab sources (Ra-226 αβγ, Am-241 αγ, Sr-90 β, Co-60 γ - Table 25.3). Precautions: forceps, don’t point, lead box, wash hands, warning signs. Background = environmental ionizing radiation. Corrected count rate = recorded − background (full treatment with GM in 25.3). Hong Kong ~1 count s⁻¹ (stated with the GM counter).
- **Check:** 2021/33 (sea water / rock / human body). QB `PHY15011116`, `PHY15011117`. Textbook Checkpoint on background T/F.

## 25.3 Properties of nuclear radiation (printed pp.27–44)

### A. Ionizing power

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.24 ion-pair. Table 25.4 speed / mass / charge. Fig. 25.25 ion-pairs along a path. Fig. 25.26 conducting gas.
- **Text:** α slow, heavy, +2 → strongest ionizing power; γ weakest. Book values (ion-pairs per cm in air): α 20 000–50 000; β 50–500; γ 5–8. Inverse link to range and penetrating power comes next.
- **Check:** HKDSE 2016/32 (1); PP 2012/34 ionizing + discharge of a charged sphere. Example 25.5 (current with α source).

### Detection

- **Visual:** Cloud chamber (Fig. 25.27 + Table 25.5 tracks). Film badge (Fig. 25.29). GM tube internals (Fig. 25.30) plus corrected count rate formula.
- **Text:** Cloud chamber visualizes tracks (α thick straight; β thin irregular; γ faint / scattered). Film blackening ~ dose; absorbers identify type; α not seen if paper wraps the film. GM: argon ion-pairs → pulses; better for α, β than γ; remove plastic grid for α. Count rate fluctuates (random). Always subtract background.
- **Check:** QB `PHY15011111` (evacuate chamber → no tracks), `PHY15011118` / `PHY15012116` film badge. HKDSE 2026/12(b) ring dosimeter (same idea as badge).

### Range in air and penetrating power

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.31 ranges. Fig. 25.32 paper / 5 mm Al / 25 mm Pb. Absorber experiment (Example 25.6 table).
- **Text:** Stronger ionizing → shorter range. In air: α ~5 cm, β ~5 m, γ ~100 m. Paper stops α; ~5 mm Al stops β; 25 mm Pb halves γ (does not zero it). Identify sources by significant count-rate drops, never below background unless the shield also blocks background.
- **Check:** HKDSE 2014/32, 2019/31, sap/36. Textbook Checkpoint 8 absorber table.

### D. Electric and magnetic deflection

- **Visual:** Fig. 25.33 E-field. Fig. 25.34 B into page. Fleming left-hand rule sidebar (β travels opposite to I).
- **Text:** α toward negative plate / one B sense; β opposite and larger deflection (tiny mass, ~1/7200 of α). γ undeflected. Vacuum so α is not stopped in air. Collimator = lead with a slit.
- **Check:** HKDSE 2017/31 (E-field diagrams). PP q35 (GM tubes X and Y in B). QB `PHY15013101` / `PHY15013104` as worked examples.
